# Messaging Guardrails

**Read this file before producing any Persado deliverable.** These guardrails are not suggestions. They are hard constraints developed through extensive iteration to prevent the specific failure modes that have weakened Persado's messaging in the past. Every rule here exists because we've seen the violation in real output and know the damage it causes.

---

## SECTION 1: BANNED LANGUAGE

These words and phrases are prohibited in all Persado go-to-market content. If you find yourself reaching for any of them, it's a signal that the underlying message isn't specific enough.

### Banned Filler Words
| Word / Phrase | Why It's Banned | What to Write Instead |
|---------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| "leverage" | Corporate filler. Means nothing specific. | Name the specific action: "use," "deploy," "replace," "redirect" |
| "empower" | Vague. What power? To do what? | Name the specific outcome: "lets your team ship in days" |
| "cutting-edge" | Undifferentiated. Every AI vendor claims this. | Name what makes it different: "purpose-built for regulated industries" |
| "state-of-the-art" | Same as above. Empty superlative. | Replace with a specific proof point |
| "next-generation" | Meaningless modifier. Generation of what? | Delete. The sentence is stronger without it. |
| "innovative" | Self-congratulatory. The buyer decides if it's innovative. | Show the innovation through the outcome, don't label it |
| "best-in-class" | Unverifiable claim. Sounds like a brochure. | Use a specific stat: "96% win rate vs. human and LLM content" |
| "robust" | Vague intensifier. What's robust about it? | Be specific: "20+ regulatory frameworks built into generation" |
| "seamless" | Overused in SaaS. Nobody believes this anymore. | Describe the actual integration: "Plugs into SFMC, Adobe, Braze" |
| "holistic" | Management-speak. Nobody outside a boardroom talks like this. | Name the specific scope: "covers email, direct mail, web, social" |
| "synergy" / "synergistic" | Never. Under any circumstances. | Describe the actual relationship between the things |
| "solution" (as a standalone noun) | Too vague. What solution? To what problem? | Name the specific product: "Persado CREATE," "the platform" |
| "unlock" (as in "unlock value") | Overused, vague. What value? How? | "Redirect $525K from agency retainers to media and strategy" |
| "harness" (as in "harness AI") | Cliché. Tells the buyer nothing. | Name what the AI does: "generates compliant content in hours" |
| "revolutionize" | Hyperbole. Erodes trust. | Let the numbers speak: "4× faster, 75% cheaper" |
| "transform" / "transformative" | Overused in AI marketing. Means everything and nothing. | Describe the before/after specifically |
| "end-to-end" | Vague unless immediately followed by specifics. | "From brief to coded, QA'd, deployment-ready asset" |
| "at scale" (without specifying the scale) | Empty without a number. | "120K+ campaigns," "600K sends per credit," "8/10 largest U.S. banks" |

### Banned Phrases and Constructions

**"Better, Faster, Compliant"**
Why: Every vendor claims this. Tells nobody anything. The messaging needs to name the specific trade-off being dissolved and the specific budget being displaced.
Instead: "What takes your agency 6 weeks, we deliver in days — at 75% lower cost, fully compliant."

**"AI-powered [anything]"**
Why: Undifferentiated. In 2026, every content tool is AI-powered. This is like saying "internet-enabled" in 2010.
Instead: Name the specific outcome the AI produces: "production-ready assets," "compliance-validated content," "performance-predicted variants."

**"Persado uses advanced natural language generation and machine learning models trained on millions of A/B tests to optimize marketing content."**
Why: This exact sentence (or variants of it) is the single most common messaging failure. It leads with technology, explains architecture, and doesn't answer "so what?" from the buyer's perspective. It is the canonical example of what NOT to write.
Instead: "We help you cut agency production costs by 75% while getting to market 4× faster — without adding compliance risk. That's budget you can redirect to media and strategy."

**"Compliance-first" as a lead**
Why: Sounds defensive, not aspirational. Doesn't create urgency. Compliance is Layer 3 in the hierarchy — it removes the objection. It doesn't hook.
Instead: Lead with speed, compound with cost, THEN introduce compliance as the answer to "but is it safe?"

**"Our AI / Our platform / Our technology"**
Why: Prospect doesn't care about your AI. They care about their problem. "Our" language centers Persado instead of the buyer.
Instead: "Your team ships campaigns in days instead of weeks." "Your agency spend drops by 75%." Center the buyer's outcome.

## SECTION 2: STRUCTURAL RULES

These rules govern how content is organized, not just what it says.

### Rule 1: Speed First, Always
Every piece of outreach, every pitch deck, every website section, every one-pager must lead with speed. See `/knowledge/positioning/messaging-hierarchy.md` for the full rationale. If speed is not in the first sentence or headline, restructure.

Exceptions: Content specifically written for compliance officer personas may lead with compliance, but should still frame it through the lens of "compliance without slowing down" — not "compliance for compliance's sake."

### Rule 2: One Primary Hook Per Section
Every section, slide, email, or page should have ONE primary message. Not two. Not three. The instinct to "say everything" dilutes everything. Pick the single most relevant hook for that audience and context, and make it land.

The identity crisis problem: Is Persado a compliance tool? A content platform? A personalization engine? The answer is "yes, all three" — but no individual section should try to sell all three simultaneously. Each section earns the right to introduce the next benefit.

### Rule 3: Outcomes Before Technology
Every headline answers "so what?" from the buyer's perspective. If a sentence explains HOW Persado works (technology, architecture, models) before explaining WHAT the buyer gets (speed, savings, compliance, performance), flip the order. In most cases, the "how" can be cut entirely.

**Before (technology-first):** "Persado's regulatory-aware AI generates high-performing content with performance predictions built in."
**After (outcome-first):** "Brief to production-ready assets in 3-5 days. Fully compliant. Performance-scored before they ship."

The buyer doesn't need to understand the technology to want the outcome. Technology explanations belong in deep-dive sections, technical appendices, or responses to "how does it work?" questions — never in hooks, headlines, or opening paragraphs.

### Rule 4: Specific Numbers Beat Adjectives
Every time you're tempted to write "faster," write "4× faster." Every time you want to say "cheaper," write "75% lower cost." Every time you want to say "better," write "96% win rate." Adjectives are claims. Numbers are evidence.

| Don't Write | Do Write |
|-------------|----------|
| "Faster campaigns" | "Campaigns in days, not weeks" or "4× faster to market" |
| "Lower costs" | "75% lower production cost" or "$4-5K per asset vs. $10-15K" |
| "Better performance" | "96% win rate vs. human-written content" or "+43% conversion lift" |
| "Compliant content" | "100% compliant. Zero incidents. 20+ frameworks built in." |
| "Significant time savings" | "87% time reduction. 345 hours saved per year per campaign." |
| "Leading banks" | "8 of 10 largest U.S. banks" |
| "Extensive experience" | "10+ years. 1M+ A/B tests. 120K+ campaigns." |

### Rule 5: Name the Trade-Off Being Dissolved
Persado's core positioning is the dissolution of a false choice. If a piece of content doesn't name the trade-off (speed vs. compliance, cost vs. quality, automation vs. control), it's missing the most compelling part of the argument.

**The false choice:** Financial services marketers believe they must choose between moving fast (risking regulatory violations) and staying safe (missing market windows).

**The dissolution:** Persado eliminates this choice. Speed and compliance are built together, not bolted on sequentially. You don't "balance" them — you get both, fully, because of the architecture.

**Never imply Persado helps buyers "balance" or "manage" the trade-off.** The word is "dissolve." Persado doesn't make the trade-off easier — it makes it disappear.

### Rule 6: Name the Budget Being Displaced
Persado doesn't add to a buyer's tech stack cost. It displaces existing spend. Every business case should identify what budget Persado replaces:

- CREATE displaces: Agency production retainers ($1M+)
- OPTIMIZE displaces: Testing/optimization tool spend + wasted media spend on underperforming creative
- AUTOMATE displaces: Manual ESP versioning labor + template management overhead

The framing: "This isn't new budget. It's redirected budget. You're already spending this money on slower, more expensive, less compliant production."

### Rule 7: Don't Pitch All Three Plays at Once
This is a recurring structural failure. The instinct is to show the full breadth of the platform. The result is dilution. Each play targets a different buyer with a different pain point and a different budget.

Diagnose first: "How long does it take to get a campaign to market?" → If speed/cost is primary pain → CREATE. "What percentage gets rejected by compliance?" → If optimization is primary pain → OPTIMIZE. "How many hours does CRM personalization take?" → If automation is primary pain → AUTOMATE.

One play, executed well, beats three plays pitched generically.

### Rule 8: Compliance Is "During Generation, Not After"
This phrase is a messaging weapon. Use it prominently and consistently. It captures Persado's architectural difference in seven words, and it immediately reframes the compliance conversation from "can your AI pass review?" to "our AI doesn't need a review cycle."

Never write: "Persado includes compliance features" or "compliance checking is available" or "content is reviewed for compliance." These framings imply compliance is a step that happens to content. The correct framing is that compliance is embedded in the generation process itself — the content was never non-compliant to begin with.

**The architectural difference (use visually where possible):**

Generic AI / Agencies: Generate → Review → Reject → Revise → Review → ... (6-12 week cycle)
Persado: Generate + Validate → Deploy (Days)

## SECTION 3: THE SPECIFICITY TEST

Before any deliverable is final, apply this test: **If you remove "Persado" from the copy, could it describe any other AI content tool?** If the answer is yes, the content is too generic and needs to be rewritten.

Persado's regulated-industry specialization must be undeniable in every piece of content. Generic AI positioning is the single biggest ongoing risk to Persado's messaging. Here's how to pressure-test for it:

### Fail Examples (Too Generic)
- "AI-powered content creation for better marketing results" → Could describe Jasper, Copy.ai, any of 50 tools
- "Generate high-performing content faster" → Could describe literally any AI writing tool
- "Our platform helps marketers create content at scale" → Zero differentiation
- "Use AI to improve your marketing campaigns" → Could be an ad for ChatGPT

### Pass Examples (Persado-Specific)
- "Brief to production-ready, compliance-validated assets in 3-5 days — coded, QA'd, and deployment-ready"
- "The only platform where compliance validation happens during generation, not after — zero revision cycles"
- "Trained on 10 years of financial services data, 1M+ A/B tests, and 20+ regulatory frameworks"
- "Replace your $1M agency production retainer with a system that's 4× faster and pre-validated for UDAAP, TILA, Reg Z, and ECOA"

The difference: specificity. Naming the regulatory frameworks. Naming "production-ready" (not "drafts"). Naming the budget being displaced. Naming the time compression. These details are what make the messaging inimitable.

## SECTION 4: THE "SO WHAT?" TEST

Every claim, every headline, every bullet point must survive this question: **"So what? Why should a VP of Marketing at a top-10 bank care about this specific thing?"**

If the claim can't answer that question clearly and immediately, it fails the test.

### Examples

| Claim | "So What?" Answer | Verdict |
|-------|-------------------|---------|
| "Persado uses NLG models" | "So what? Every AI tool does." | ❌ FAIL — technology, not outcome |
| "4× faster to market" | "So what? → I can respond to rate changes before competitors. I can hit seasonal windows. I can stop missing launch dates." | ✓ PASS — names a pain the buyer feels |
| "1M+ A/B tests" | "So what? → It means the performance predictions are trained on real outcomes, not synthetic data. The system knows what actually converts in my industry." | ✓ PASS — but only if the "so what" is made explicit in the copy |
| "AI-generated content" | "So what? ChatGPT does that for free." | ❌ FAIL — undifferentiated |
| "Production-ready assets" | "So what? → I don't need to route through design, dev, and QA. The asset ships as-is." | ✓ PASS — names a step that gets eliminated |
| "Compliance during generation" | "So what? → No revision cycles. No 3-week compliance bottleneck. The thing that makes campaigns slow doesn't exist." | ✓ PASS — dissolves the trade-off |

**Rule of thumb:** If a proof point or claim needs a second sentence to explain why it matters, include that second sentence. A stat without context is just a number. "1M+ A/B tests" is a stat. "1M+ A/B tests — which means our performance predictions correlate at 96% with actual results" is evidence.

## SECTION 5: AUDIENCE-SPECIFIC GUARDRAILS

Different buyers require different emphasis, but ALL must still follow Speed > Cost > Compliance > Performance.

### For CMOs / Creative Budget Owners
- Lead with: Speed and cost (agency displacement)
- Emphasize: Budget reallocation from production to strategy
- De-emphasize: Technical architecture, compliance details (they trust their compliance team to evaluate)
- Tone: Strategic, ROI-focused, boardroom-ready
- Framing: "Redirect $1M from agency execution to growth initiatives"

### For Marketing Ops Leaders
- Lead with: Speed and performance (testing velocity)
- Emphasize: Compound ROI on existing approved assets, no compliance reset
- De-emphasize: Agency displacement (not their budget), pricing details
- Tone: Operational, data-driven, efficiency-focused
- Framing: "Improve results without retriggering compliance cycles"

### For CRM / SFMC Owners
- Lead with: Time savings and automation
- Emphasize: 400 hrs → 55 hrs, set-once-run-forever, 600K sends per credit
- De-emphasize: Creative quality arguments (not their concern), agency model
- Tone: Technical-operational, integration-focused
- Framing: "Set up once. Personalize forever. No manual versioning."

### For Compliance Officers (Influencers, Not Buyers)
- Lead with: Compliance architecture (exception to the hierarchy — this audience needs compliance first)
- Emphasize: 20+ frameworks, full audit trail, zero incidents, controlled variation
- De-emphasize: Speed and cost (sounds like cutting corners to a compliance person)
- Tone: Rigorous, evidence-based, conservative
- Framing: "Every asset includes a full compliance audit trail. Validation happens during generation, not after. You review a confirmation, not a first draft."
- **Critical:** Never imply speed comes at the expense of compliance rigor. To a compliance officer, speed = risk. Persado's entire value to this persona is that speed does NOT equal risk.

### For CFOs / Finance (In Business Cases)
- Lead with: Cost displacement and ROI
- Emphasize: Agency retainer replacement math, payback period, redirectable budget
- De-emphasize: Performance lift (hard to forecast), creative quality
- Tone: Financial, conservative, evidence-based
- Framing: "50 agency assets × $15K = $750K. Persado: 50 credits × $4.5K = $225K. Save $525K. Payback in Q1."
- **Note:** The $2.5B incremental value claim needs full context for this audience: "measured incremental revenue between Persado-generated content vs. control groups, across all client communications, over 4 years."

## SECTION 6: COMPETITIVE POSITIONING GUARDRAILS

### vs. Traditional Agencies (Publicis, Havas, VML, McCann)
- **Do:** Frame as agency displacement, not agency replacement. "Keep your agency for strategy. Use Persado for production."
- **Do:** Name the cost and time comparisons explicitly: 6-8 weeks → days, $10-15K per asset → $4-5K.
- **Don't:** Attack agencies broadly. Many prospects have personal relationships with agency contacts.
- **Don't:** Claim Persado does strategy. It doesn't. It does production, compliance, optimization.

### vs. Generic AI (ChatGPT, Jasper, Copy.ai)
- **Do:** Frame the comparison as "drafts vs. production-ready." "They give you text. We give you coded, QA'd, deployment-ready assets."
- **Do:** Hammer the compliance gap. Zero compliance frameworks built into generic AI. Every output requires manual legal review.
- **Do:** Use the analogy: "That's like saying you have a word processor, so why hire an agency? The question isn't can you generate words — it's can you generate compliant, high-performing content at scale."
- **Don't:** Dismiss generic AI tools entirely. Prospects use and like them. Acknowledge they're useful for first-draft ideation. Then reframe: "The question is what happens between the draft and production."
- **Don't:** Get into a model architecture comparison. Nobody cares which LLM is under the hood. The differentiator is the compliance layer, the performance data, and the production-ready output.

### vs. Martech / Personalization (Movable Ink, Jacquard)
- **Do:** Differentiate on scope: widget-level vs. full-asset creation.
- **Do:** Differentiate on compliance: no compliance layer in these tools.
- **Don't:** Position as a direct replacement unless the specific use case warrants it.

### vs. Optimization / Testing (Optimizely, VWO)
- **Do:** Frame as upstream vs. downstream: "They test content. We create the content worth testing."
- **Don't:** Dismiss testing platforms. Position Persado as what feeds them better variants.

## SECTION 7: TONE AND VOICE

### What Persado Sounds Like
- **Confident, not arrogant.** We state facts and numbers. We don't claim we're the best — we show the evidence and let the buyer decide.
- **Specific, not vague.** Every claim includes a number, a timeframe, or a named comparison.
- **Direct, not diplomatic.** Short sentences. Active voice. No hedge-padding.
- **Buyer-centric, not product-centric.** "Your team ships in days" not "Our platform enables faster deployment."
- **Urgent, not panicked.** There's a real cost to inaction (missed windows, wasted spend, competitive disadvantage), and we name it — but we don't manufacture fear.

### What Persado Does NOT Sound Like
- A technical whitepaper (no jargon-first explanations)
- A startup pitch deck (no hyperbole, no "revolutionary")
- A compliance document (no defensive, hedge-everything language)
- A generic SaaS vendor (no "seamless," "robust," "holistic," "unlock")
- An agency capabilities deck (no "our team of experts," "bespoke approach")

### Sentence-Level Standards
- Prefer active voice: "Persado delivers" not "assets are delivered by Persado"
- Prefer short sentences: If a sentence has two commas and an em-dash, it's probably two sentences
- Prefer concrete nouns: "coded HTML email" not "digital asset"
- One idea per sentence: Don't compound speed, cost, and compliance into a single clause unless you're using the canonical one-liner
- Kill modifiers that don't add information: "very," "really," "significantly," "highly"

## SECTION 8: VISUAL AND LAYOUT GUARDRAILS

### Text Overflow
No text overflow. Ever. If content doesn't fit the spatial budget of a slide, section, or template, cut words — don't shrink fonts, don't stretch boxes, don't overflow into margins. The copy must be edited to fit, not the layout stretched to accommodate.

### Proof Points in Headlines
The most compelling numbers (96% win rate, 90% fewer rejections, 72hr to production, 4× faster, 75% cheaper) belong in headlines and hero sections — not buried in body copy. If a proof point is in a paragraph, ask: should this be a headline instead?

### The Credentials Bar
Use this pattern on hero sections, landing pages, and deck openers:
**10+ years | 8/10 largest U.S. banks | $2.5B+ incremental value | Zero compliance incidents**

### Comparison Tables
When comparing Persado to alternatives, always use the same column structure:
**Speed → Cost → Compliance → Output → Performance → Learning**
This forces the comparison to follow the messaging hierarchy and ensures Persado's advantages are presented in the right order.

## SECTION 9: QUICK-REFERENCE DECISION TREE

Use this when you're unsure whether a piece of content is on-strategy:

```
Does it lead with speed?
├── YES → Does cost come second?
│   ├── YES → Does compliance remove the objection (not lead)?
│   │   ├── YES → Does performance close (not open)?
│   │   │   ├── YES → Does it name a specific trade-off being dissolved?
│   │   │   │   ├── YES → Does it name the budget being displaced?
│   │   │   │   │   ├── YES → Does it pass the specificity test?
│   │   │   │   │   │   ├── YES → Does every claim pass "so what?"
│   │   │   │   │   │   │   ├── YES → ✓ Ship it.
│   │   │   │   │   │   │   └── NO → Rewrite the failing claims.
│   │   │   │   │   │   └── NO → Add Persado-specific detail.
│   │   │   │   │   └── NO → Add budget displacement math.
│   │   │   │   └── NO → Add the false choice framing.
│   │   │   └── NO → Move performance to the end.
│   │   └── NO → Restructure compliance as objection-removal.
│   └── NO → Move cost to second position.
└── NO → Restructure. Speed must lead.
```
