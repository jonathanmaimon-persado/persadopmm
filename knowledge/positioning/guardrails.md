# Messaging Guardrails

Read this before writing any deliverable. These rules are non-negotiable.

## NEVER SAY

### Technology-First Language
- "Persado uses advanced natural language generation and machine learning models trained on millions of A/B tests to optimize marketing content."
- Any explanation that leads with model architecture, training data, or AI capabilities
- "Our AI engine," "our models," "our algorithms" as the subject of a sentence

### Weak/Generic Positioning
- "Compliance-first" as the lead message (defensive, not aspirational)
- "Better, Faster, Compliant" (vague — every vendor claims this)
- Leading with performance claims before establishing speed, cost, and compliance credibility

### Banned Words and Phrases
- "Leverage"
- "Empower"
- "Cutting-edge"
- "State-of-the-art"
- "Next-generation"
- "Revolutionary"
- "Game-changing"
- "Unlock"
- "Harness the power of"

### The Genericity Trap
- Any headline that could describe another AI content tool if you removed "Persado"
- Generic AI benefit statements: "Create better content faster with AI"
- Feature descriptions without regulated-industry context

## ALWAYS SAY

### Follow the Messaging Hierarchy
1. Lead with **speed** (the pain they feel daily)
2. Compound with **cost** (the business case)
3. Remove the **compliance** objection
4. Close with **performance**

### Business Outcomes, Not Technology
- "Days instead of weeks" — not "natural language generation"
- "75% lower cost" — not "AI-powered content creation"
- "Zero compliance incidents" — not "built-in regulatory framework analysis"

### Specificity Over Adjectives
- Use numbers: 4x, 75%, 96%, $525K saved
- Name the specific trade-off being dissolved
- Name the specific budget being displaced
- Reference specific regulatory frameworks (UDAAP, TILA, Reg Z)

### Key Phrases to Use Prominently
- **"Compliance during generation, not after"** — this single phrase is worth paragraphs of explanation
- **"The only way to move fast without adding risk"** — the central thesis
- **"Production-ready, not drafts"** — the key differentiator vs. generic AI
- **"Days, not weeks"** — the speed hook
- **"Brief to production-ready in 72 hours"** — speed with specificity

## QUALITY TESTS

### The "So What?" Test
Every claim, headline, and bullet must survive: **"So what? Why should a VP of Marketing at a bank care about this specific thing?"**

If it can't answer that clearly, rewrite it.

**Fails the test:** "Persado generates optimized marketing content using AI."
**Passes the test:** "Your spring card acquisition campaign — live in days, not months. 75% less than your agency charges."

### The Specificity Test
If you remove "Persado" from the copy and it could describe any AI content tool, it's too generic.

**Fails the test:** "AI-powered content creation for better marketing results."
**Passes the test:** "The only creative system built for regulated industries — 20+ compliance frameworks validated during generation, not reviewed after."

### The Hierarchy Test
Does the piece lead with speed? Does cost follow naturally? Is compliance positioned as dissolving the trade-off (not as a feature)? Does performance close?

If any of these are out of order, restructure.

## COMMON MISTAKES TO CATCH

1. **Opening with performance** — "96% win rate" means nothing to a stranger. Speed opens doors.
2. **Treating compliance as a feature** — It's not a checkbox. It's the dissolution of the fundamental trade-off in regulated marketing.
3. **Generic AI language** — If ChatGPT could make the same claim, it's not specific enough.
4. **Technology explanations** — Nobody buys "natural language generation." They buy "days instead of weeks."
5. **Listing all three plays at once** — Diagnose first, lead with one. One play well-executed beats three plays poorly pitched.
