# Voice and Tone

## Brand Voice Attributes

### Confident, Not Arrogant
- State capabilities as facts with proof points
- Let numbers speak instead of superlatives
- "96% win rate" — not "the best AI content platform ever built"

### Direct, Not Aggressive
- Get to the point fast. Every sentence earns its place.
- Name competitors and alternatives directly when appropriate (agencies, ChatGPT)
- Don't hedge — if Persado is faster, say "4x faster," not "significantly faster"

### Specific, Not Vague
- Numbers over adjectives, always
- Name the regulation, the framework, the specific pain point
- "$4-5K per asset vs. $10-15K from your agency" — not "dramatically lower costs"

### Expert, Not Academic
- Speak like a senior PMM who knows the industry, not a professor explaining it
- Reference real workflows: "Your compliance team reviews it. Finds issues. Sends it back. You fix it. They review again. That's 3 weeks."
- Use industry language naturally: SFMC, UDAAP, LOB, ESP

### Provocative, Not Hostile
- Challenge the status quo: "Why are you still paying agency rates for content that AI produces better?"
- Reframe the conversation: agencies are the competitor, not other AI tools
- Never disparage prospects' current decisions — acknowledge the logic, then show the better path

## Tone Adjustments by Context

### Outreach (Cold Email, LinkedIn)
- Punchy, direct, short sentences
- Lead with a specific pain point or provocative question
- No more than 3-4 sentences per email
- Subject lines under 50 characters

### Pitch Decks
- Confident and authoritative
- Heavy on proof points and visuals
- Minimal text per slide — let the presenter fill the gaps
- Structure follows messaging hierarchy exactly

### One-Pagers / Collateral
- Scannable, not readable — bullet points over paragraphs
- Key stat in the largest font
- Vertical-specific where possible
- CTA that's easy and low-commitment

### Website Copy
- Conversational but professional
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
- Every section answers "so what?"
- Progressive disclosure: headline hooks, body explains, CTA acts

### Sales Enablement
- Practical and tactical
- Written for salespeople to use, not for marketers to admire
- Discovery questions, not scripts
- Objection responses that are conversational, not robotic

## Words We Use

| Instead of... | Say... |
|---------------|--------|
| Leverage | Use |
| Utilize | Use |
| Empower | Enable / Give |
| Cutting-edge | Purpose-built |
| State-of-the-art | Proven (with proof point) |
| Next-generation | Specific capability description |
| Solution | System / Platform / (specific product name) |
| Synergy | (just don't) |
| Optimize (as vague verb) | Specific action: "improve click rates," "reduce rework" |

## Writing Rules

1. **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence has a comma and "and," split it.
2. **Active voice.** "Persado generates compliant content" — not "Compliant content is generated by Persado."
3. **No throat-clearing.** Delete "In today's competitive landscape" and "As the financial services industry evolves." Start with the point.
4. **Tight > comprehensive.** 80% scope at 100% quality beats 100% scope at 80% quality.
5. **Every headline survives the "so what?" test.** No exceptions.
